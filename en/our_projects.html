For the community

Our original projects

Besides flying, we are always building and tinkering. Soldering, assembling drones, modelling and 3D printing are all part of the hobby. Open-source projects keep FPV moving forward. Here you can find solutions created by our club members that may also come in handy on your own FPV journey.

Modular Gate System
("M-Gate")

A modular gate system for Whoops and other small drones, with connectors designed to link the gates into 2D and 3D configurations.

The gates are built from simple LED tubes using addressable WLEDs, 3D-printed connectors and a simple controller based on an ESP module. After a few assembly and disassembly sessions, the whole system can be set up or packed away in just a few minutes, while individual gates can be stored in a small cardboard box.

This is the system we use at all our sessions.

Designer: Chicken

HEX add-on for the M-Gate system

This is an add-on to the basic M-Gate System. However, you don't need any of 3D-printed parts from the basic version to use it.

It uses the same 20 mm LED tubes, with additional 1/2" tubes fitted into the hexagonal mounts. These reinforce the structure and allow the tunnel to be positioned precisely.

Designer: Chicken

PVC tube connectors for RaceGoW tracks

The project includes 3D models of all the connectors needed to build a track that follows the RaceGOW competition rules.

The connectors are designed for 1/2" PVC tubing, with the option to slightly adjust the hole sizes for a perfect fit. While PVC tubing is readily available at pretty much any hardware store, some of the more unusual connectors can be much harder to find — hence the idea for a custom design.

An ideal solution for anyone who wants to quickly and easily build a private track at home for individual training and rack up some serious "stick time".

Designer: Gordo

SKADIS 1S battery organiser

This small organiser lets you store your batteries on an IKEA SKÅDIS pegboard alongside the rest of your FPV gear. It has been tested and is compatible with BetaFPV LAVA series batteries. It should also work with other batteries measuring 10 × 6 mm in cross-section.

Here are some of its features:

  • Ergonomic design — the angled position of the batteries makes them easy to insert and remove.
  • Oszczędność miejsca - kątowe ustawienie akumulatorów pozwala na ciaśniejsze upakowanie kilku organizerów w pionie,
  • Space saving — the angled layout allows multiple organisers to be packed more tightly together vertically.
  • Stylish design — with a slight sci-fi vibe. :)
  • Charged/uncharged — each slot lets you place the battery in one of two positions. You can use this to tell charged batteries from uncharged ones — an age-old problem for FPV pilots, right?

Designer: Gordo

C03 camera canopy

This project was created to fix some of the issues with the original Air65 Whoop canopy — namely the camera shifting after crashes or when the rear screw is tightened too much. Gordo was getting seriously annoyed with it, so he designed an alternative canopy and spent two months refining and testing it at training sessions and races.

You can choose the canopy depending on the camera angle you fly:

  • 50 degrees
  • 45 degrees
  • 42.5 degrees
  • 40 degrees
  • 35 degrees
The 45-degree version weighs around 0.82 g after printing, while the others can weigh up to 0.93 g.

Designer: Gordo

Canopy for the BetaFPV Air65 Whoop

Canopy designed for:

  • durability
  • VTX antenna connector protection
  • speed - the camera angle is set to 45 degrees
  • improved camera and antenna mounting - tight-fitting mounting holes
  • reducing stress on the drone frame through carefully designed flex points
  • ease of printing

Designer: Gordo

Case for the Eachine Pro58 module for Skyzone O4 and Eachine EV3000 goggles

A housing for the Eachine Pro58 analogue receiver module, compatible with:

  • Skyzone O4X
  • Skyzone O40
  • Skyzone O4L
  • Eachine EV3000

The module snap-fits into place, but requires a 9-pin extension cable to connect the receiver to the goggles. PLA Tough+ or PLA is recommended for printing.

Designer: Chicken

Sci-Fi case for the PhobosLT lap timer

A sci-fi-looking housing for a laptimer - a system for measuring lap times.

The 3D model was designed to house the electronics based on a project by Phobos who also wrote the software for the system.

The advantage of using a lap timer during home training is that you can track your progress — plus you get +1000 to coolness when you hear voice commentary on your lap times. :)

Designer: Gordo
