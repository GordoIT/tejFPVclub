Welcome to the big world of tiny drones

What is a Tiny Whoop?

A Tiny Whoop is a very small and lightweight multirotor designed mainly for indoor flying. Most models have propeller ducts, which make them much safer to fly around people and reduce the risk of damaging things in a crash. Control usually via 2.4 GHz radio, while the video feed is transmitted to the pilot's goggles at 5.8 GHz.

Why Tiny Whoops?

  • relatively low cost of entry comapred to larger drones, such as 5-inch
  • easy and inexpensive to repair yourself, with plenty of spare parts available on the market
  • with no flight assistance from the onboard computer, you have to rely entirely on your own technique and reflexes. It's a great way to develop your manual flying skills
  • you also learn a thing or two about physics and electronics
  • teaches discipline and perserverance
  • an amazing community
  • no aviation licence is required for indoor flying
  • you can fly at home regardless of the weather

Whoop of Poland League

Tiny Whoops really took off in Poland in 2020, when Hubert Wilczyński (aka Hubus) started the Whoop od Poland League which organizes ten races each year in different cities across Poland. So far, it has visited Warsaw, Szczecin, Wrocław, Radomsko, Olsztyn, Gdańsk, Lubiąż, Olsztyn Jurajski, Łódź, Bydgoszcz and, of course, Poznań.

History of the Tiny Whoop

The first Tiny Whoop was created in the United States in 2015, as a result of experiments by pilots from Team Big Whoop in Colorado, who were competing in larger drone races at the time. For fun, they started modifying micro drones that were available in shops, such as the Blade Inductrix from Horizon Hobby. By adding miniature cameras and video transmitters, they turned them into harmless little drones weighing up to 30 g and fitted with 65 mm propellers. That same year, Jesse Perkins, who came up with the whole idea, started a YouTube channel and, a year later, launched the TinyWhoop.com online store. Over the years, the name "Tiny Whoop" became synonymous with indoor racing, which has attracted fans all over the world.

What is RaceGoW?

RaceGOW is a series of eight "mini UTTs" (Universal Time Trials) — small FPV race tracks designed to fit in almost any home. Each RaceGOW track fits within a space of approximately 120 × 180 cm, using the minimum permitted gate size of 24 inches. During the season, a new track is released every two weeks, along with a video showing how to build it. Pilots then fly the track following the official flight video provided by the creator and league host, Dan Sugano, aka FPV Skittles. The goal is to achieve the fastest time over three consecutive laps, then submit your DVR footage and result through the RaceGOW.com website.

What is IGoW?

The International Game of Whoop is the world's largest online league and competition for Tiny Whoop pilots. Every week, participants are given a new challenge — sometimes it’s a trick to perform, sometimes a track to build, and sometimes a creative FPV flying challenge.
IGoW combines competition, flying skills and a lot of fun, which has made it one of the most interesting initiatives in the Tiny Whoop community.

How to prepare for a training session


Set up your channels

When flying together, we use the same channels as during the races: R1, R3, R6 and R7. Program these channels on your drone and set up your radio so you can switch between them easily.

Set your VTX power

Among Tiny Whoop pilots, the standard for flying together is 25 mW of video transmission power. Using higher values can interfere with the video signal of other pilots leaving them blind.

Set your VTX power accordingly so you don't interfere with anyone else.

Pack the essentials

Here's a list of things worth bringing for the session, in addition to your drone, goggles, radio and charged batteries:

  • screwdriver
  • a set of spare propellers (preferably several)
  • a laptop and USB cable for connecting your drone
  • LiPo battery charger
  • a portable soldering iron (very useful)
  • spare parts (motors, canopy, frame)
